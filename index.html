Missed a session? Swap / skip rules

  • Missed easy day — skip it. Don’t stack two easies the next day.
  • Missed quality — don’t double up. Do an easy run instead, resume normal next quality day.
  • Missed long run — shorten next long run slightly, or shift it one day if recovered. Never “make up” full long + quality in 48 hours.
  • Sick / injured / life chaos — use Easy week on that week after generating (or keep only easy runs until you’re healthy).

What are strides? Short, smooth accelerations — usually 15–30 seconds (or ~100 m) at about 85–90% effort, not an all-out sprint. Stay relaxed (shoulders, hands, face). Jog or walk easy for 45–90 seconds between each. They wake up the legs and improve form without hard training stress. Typical set: 4–6 × 20–30s near the end of an easy run.

Keep easy days easy

Most fitness is built on aerobic work. If “easy” runs are actually hard, you stack fatigue without getting faster.

Ego out. Lifetime in.

Training is not about impressing people on Strava or in the group. It’s about building fitness you still have in 10 years.

When easy runs creep up in heart rate and pace, you get extra neuromuscular fatigue and stress that should be saved for quality sessions. The plan’s hard days stop working. Sleep, mood, and injury risk get worse.

Rule of thumb: on easy days you should be able to speak in full sentences. If you’re gasping, it’s not easy — slow down or walk the hills.

Maffetone method (MAF heart rate)

Dr Phil Maffetone’s simple ceiling for maximum aerobic effort: stay at or under this heart rate on easy/aerobic days so you train the aerobic system instead of constantly dipping into higher stress.

Suggested max aerobic HR (MAF)
145 bpm

Aerobic sweet spot: 135–145 bpm (ceiling down to −10)

Formula: 180 − age, then apply the adjustment. Keep easy runs at or under the ceiling.

  • Use a chest strap if you can — wrist HR is often late or noisy.
  • Hills: slow down or walk so HR doesn’t spike over MAF.
  • At first the pace may feel embarrassingly slow. That’s normal. Speed at that HR improves over months.
  • Quality days in this app are separate — those are where harder effort belongs.

Other ways to check “easy”

Talk test

You can speak full sentences without gasping. Singing is optional; chatting is the bar.

Pace from this app

Easy pace is shown as roughly goal race pace + 45–75 sec/km. If that still spikes HR above MAF, follow HR and slow further.

Next-day feel

After a true easy day you should feel ready to train again — not wiped out.

Overtraining warning signs

Persistent fatigue, rising resting HR, poor sleep, irritability, stalled times, niggles that don’t settle — back off volume and intensity.

Not medical advice

Heart-rate guidelines are general coaching heuristics. If you have heart disease, take medications that affect HR, or have symptoms with exercise, talk to a doctor before using HR targets.

Warm-Up & Cool-Down

A short primer before and after you run. Drawn from practical training notes — adapt what feels right for your body.

Why Warm Up?

Skipping a warm-up invites injury. A proper warm-up primes muscles, joints, ligaments, and tendons for impact, and helps heart rate rise smoothly instead of spiking hard in the first few minutes.

Static vs Dynamic Stretching

  • Before the run — dynamic only. Moving drills (leg swings, high knees, open/close gates). Avoid holding long static stretches on cold muscles.
  • Static stretching when cold can reduce power and leave joints less stable for the work ahead.
  • After the run — gentle static stretches are fine for recovery once muscles are warm.

5-Minute Pre-Run Warm-Up

Use before easy runs, quality sessions, and long runs. Stay relaxed — this is activation, not a workout.

Time Move How
0:00–1:00Easy walkShake out arms and shoulders; breathe steadily
1:00–2:00Walk → light jogBuild to a very easy shuffle; still conversational
2:00–3:00High knees + butt kicks20–30 sec each, soft landings, upright posture
3:00–4:00Side shuffles + skips10–15 sec each direction; light skips forward
4:00–5:00Dynamic leg swings8–10 swings each leg (front/back, then side-to-side) holding a wall if needed

Optional before quality work: 2–3 progressive strides (15–20 sec smooth pickups) with full easy recovery between — not all-out sprints.

5-Minute Post-Run Cool-Down

Bring heart rate down, then gentle mobility. Save longer static holds for after you’re warm and finished.

Time Move How
0:00–2:00Easy jog → walkGradually slow to a walk; don’t stop dead after hard efforts
2:00–3:30Calf + hip flexorGentle static holds 20–30 sec each side — no bouncing, no pain
3:30–4:30Hamstring + quadSoft stretch each side; breathe out into the stretch
4:30–5:00Hips & glutesFigure-4 or seated twist, easy range only
Keep it simple. Consistently showing up beats a perfect routine. If five minutes is all you have, walk into a light jog, do a few leg swings, and start the session. Personal preference still wins — trial what leaves you feeling ready, not fatigued.

Put workouts on your watch

Use your MatRob plan sessions as custom workouts on Garmin or Apple Watch. Menus can vary slightly by model and software version.

Create a custom run workout (Garmin Connect app)

  1. Open the Garmin Connect app on your phone (watch must be paired).
  2. Tap More (bottom right).
  3. Tap Training & Planning → Workouts.
  4. Tap Create a Workout.
  5. Choose activity type: Run (or Cardio if Run isn’t listed).
  6. Build steps to match your MatRob session, for example:
    • Warm-up — time (e.g. 10–15 min) · easy effort
    • Run / Interval — distance or time + target pace if you want
    • Recover — jog or walk between reps
    • Use Repeat for sets like 8 × 400 m
    • Cool-down — easy time
  7. Tap Save, give it a clear name (e.g. “W3 Medium intervals”).
  8. Open that workout → Send to Device → pick your watch → sync.

You can also create workouts at connect.garmin.com on a computer, then send them to the watch.

Start it on the watch

  1. Open the activity (e.g. Run).
  2. Look for Training / Workouts / Workout Library (wording varies by model).
  3. Select your custom workout → start when ready.
  4. The watch will prompt you through each step (warm-up, intervals, cool-down).

Example: 8 × 400 m

1. Warm-up — 12 min · open / easy

2. Repeat × 8:

· Run — 400 m · target pace (optional)

· Recover — 90–120 sec walk/jog

3. Cool-down — 10 min easy

Create a custom workout on Apple Watch

  1. Open the Workout app on your Apple Watch.
  2. Turn the Digital Crown to Outdoor Run (or Indoor Run).
  3. Tap the ⋯ (More / Goals) button.
  4. Tap Custom, then Add (+).
  5. Build the session:
    • Warmup — Time or Distance (or Open)
    • Add → Work — time or distance for the hard bit
    • Add → Recovery — easy jog/walk between reps
    • Add more Work/Recovery pairs, or use repeats if available
    • Cooldown — Time or Distance
  6. Tap the title to name the workout (e.g. “Tempo 20 min”).
  7. Tap Create Workout.

On newer iOS / watchOS you can also build custom workouts in the Fitness app on iPhone (Workout tab → Goals → Custom); they sync to the watch.

Start it on the watch

  1. Open Workout → Outdoor Run.
  2. Your custom workouts appear under that activity.
  3. Select the one you made → start.
  4. The watch alerts you when each interval changes (haptics / chime).

Example: easy run + strides

Warmup — 10 min · Open or Time

Work — 20–40 min easy (match your plan distance/time)

Work — 20–30 sec stride · Recovery — 60–90 sec easy · repeat 4–6×

Cooldown — 5–10 min

Other devices & general tips

  • Coros / Polar / Suunto — similar idea: create structured workouts in their phone app or website, then sync to the watch.
  • No watch? Use the phone timer, or apps like Intervals.icu, Runna, or a simple interval timer. Follow the MatRob session text step by step.
  • Paces — enter the pace ranges from this app (e.g. tempo 4:30–4:40/km) as targets on Garmin/Apple when the option is available.
  • Walk/run beginners — use Work = jog time, Recovery = walk time, and set the number of repeats to match the week’s session.
  • Menus change with firmware updates; if something doesn’t match, search your brand’s help for “custom workout” or “structured workout”.
